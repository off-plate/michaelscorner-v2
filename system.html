Provenance

The orange is muscle-car orange, taken from the brand mark, and it is the recognition: it is used generously and never re-rolled. Green is the second voice. It never acts as an accent. The cream is warm because the site is meant to read as paper and receipts, not as a dashboard. Type is Clash Display for the voice, General Sans for reading, Space Mono for data and receipts only. Full rationale lives in Michael_PB/BRAND.md.

The one law

Orange acts. CTAs, the selected thing, money, brand marks.
Green speaks. Bands, secondary controls, the footer, quiet labels.
Brick is the hover, for every text link, everywhere.
Soft orange is the accent on dark and green surfaces only.

Two open decisions this table exposes

Orange on cream is 3.24:1. It carries the active nav item, the "Copy prompt" label and every small arrow, all of which are normal-size text, so they sit below AA. Brick is 5.71:1 and is already the hover colour. Changing this changes how the brand looks, so Michael decides it.

grey-2 only passes on cream. At 4.15 on cream-2 and 3.95 on sage it drops below AA the moment a card is hovered or a label sits on a sage panel. Either restrict grey-2 to the cream ground, or darken it once more so it passes on all three.

The card

There is one card. Its purpose, in one sentence: a bordered tile that groups one thing. The frame (border, radius, transition) is defined once in styles.css. A page may set only its own width, padding and inner type. If two tiles do the same job, they are the same card, and a context difference is a modifier, never a second component.

Surfaces and bands

Colour cadence is deliberate. A page alternates cream sections with at most one or two deep-green bands; two green bands never touch.

The deep-green band

Prose on green uses --green-soft. Links go pale, and the accent becomes soft orange. This is the page hero and the footer.

A secondary action ↗

The sage break

A warm interruption between two cream sections. One per viewport, never stacked against a green band.

Page hero — ONE component, used by every sub-page

Title left, intro right, so the width is used and nothing floats half empty. The homepage hero is the single documented exception.

The signature parts

The receipt — the site's one signature object. Mono carries data here, and only here.

Corner-bracket hover — the one signature gesture. Hover a card or button above.

Applied automatically to every button, link-card and summary by shared.js. It needs padding to sit outside the box, so bare text links opt out with .no-fx.

Dot-matrix icons — the entire icon vocabulary. No Lucide, no emoji.

Scroller and paddles — a full-bleed row that runs off the right edge

Cards start flush with the content gutter and bleed off the right to signal there is more. They never overlap the left. The paddles scroll it; they are hidden when everything already fits.

The one-edit test

Ask out loud before writing any CSS: "Make every primary button green instead of orange. How many places do I edit?" The answer must be one. Today, after the card merge and the token work, the frame, radius and hover of every card are each a single edit in :root. The button roles are still spread across the seven tool pages, which is the next thing to fix.

What the orange pills are telling you

The seven tool pages carry six components that exist nowhere else and are copied seven times, with 21 rule-sets duplicated byte for byte and three of them already drifted apart. That is the largest remaining piece of debt on the site, and it is why a change to a tool page currently costs seven edits. Lifting .t-hero, .face, .readout, .actions and .read-band into styles.css makes it one.
